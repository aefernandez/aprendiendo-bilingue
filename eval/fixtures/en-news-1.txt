⟦Harlow Bay⟧ council approves overhaul of ferry and bus network

After nearly two years of public meetings, surveys and more than a few heated evenings at the community hall, the ⟦Harlow Bay⟧ town council voted on Tuesday night to approve a sweeping redesign of the town's ferry and bus services. The plan, which passed ⟦7⟧ to ⟦2⟧, will replace the current timetable with buses that run every ⟦15⟧ minutes on the main routes and add an early-morning ferry crossing to ⟦Gull Island⟧ for commuters and school students.

Mayor ⟦Denise Okafor⟧ called the vote "the most important decision this council has made about how people get around since the bridge opened." Speaking to reporters after the meeting, she said the old network had been designed for a town that no longer exists. "When the routes were drawn up, most people worked at the cannery and lived within walking distance of it," she said. "Today we have nurses driving to the hospital in ⟦Eastport⟧, teenagers commuting to the college, and retirees who would happily leave the car at home if the bus came more than once an hour. We owe them a system that matches their lives."

The redesign was prepared by ⟦Northgate Transit⟧, the regional operator that runs both the buses and the ferry under contract with the town. Under the new plan, the number of bus routes will shrink from ⟦11⟧ to ⟦6⟧, but those six will run far more often. Routes that currently loop through quiet residential streets will be straightened so that buses spend less time crawling around corners and more time actually carrying passengers. In exchange, some riders will have a longer walk to their nearest stop.

That trade-off dominated the public comment period, which lasted well over an hour. ⟦Walter Lindqvist⟧, who has lived on ⟦Cedar Hill⟧ for ⟦41⟧ years, told the council that the stop outside his building would disappear. "I'm ⟦83⟧ years old," he said. "You're asking me to walk an extra ten minutes uphill to catch a bus that comes more often. That isn't much use to me if I can't get to it." Several other residents of the neighborhood made similar points, and a petition with ⟦312⟧ signatures was submitted asking the council to keep the existing loop.

In response, council members amended the plan to include a door-to-door shuttle for residents over ⟦70⟧ and people with disabilities. The shuttle can be booked by phone or online up to a day in advance and will cost the same as a regular bus fare. ⟦Priya Raman⟧, the councillor who proposed the amendment, acknowledged that it was not a perfect solution. "A shuttle you have to book the day before is not the same as a bus that simply shows up," she said. "But it means nobody on ⟦Cedar Hill⟧ is left stranded, and we'll review how it's working after six months."

The ferry changes drew less opposition. At present, the first boat to ⟦Gull Island⟧ leaves at ⟦8:15⟧ a.m., which is too late for the roughly ⟦90⟧ island residents who work early shifts on the mainland. Under the new timetable, a crossing at ⟦6:00⟧ a.m. will be added on weekdays, and the last evening return will move from ⟦7:30⟧ p.m. to ⟦9:45⟧ p.m. ⟦Tom Abernathy⟧, who runs a small bakery on the island, said the later return would make a real difference. "Right now, if I want to see a film or have dinner with friends in town, I have to leave before dessert," he said. "It sounds like a small thing, but it's the difference between feeling like you live on an island and feeling like you're stuck on one."

The overhaul will not be cheap. ⟦Northgate Transit⟧ estimates that running buses more frequently will add about $⟦1.4⟧ million a year to operating costs, mostly for additional drivers. The town will cover roughly half of that from its general budget, with the rest coming from a state grant for rural transport that ⟦Harlow Bay⟧ won last spring. Fares will stay at $⟦2⟧ for adults, while children under ⟦12⟧ will now ride for free.

The two council members who voted against the plan, ⟦Gary Whitfield⟧ and ⟦Lorraine Castillo⟧, both said they supported better transit in principle but worried about the long-term cost. "The grant runs for three years," ⟦Whitfield⟧ said. "After that, the whole bill lands on local taxpayers, and I haven't heard a convincing answer about how we'll pay it." ⟦Castillo⟧ added that she would have preferred to test the new routes on a smaller scale before committing to a full redesign.

Supporters argued that a pilot would not have shown whether the network works as a whole. ⟦Marcus Bell⟧, a transport planner at ⟦Northgate⟧, explained that frequent service only becomes useful when people can rely on it for most of their trips. "If you improve one route and leave the others alone, people still need a car for everything else, so they keep driving," he said. "The point of this plan is that you should be able to get to work, the doctor and the supermarket without checking a timetable."

Local businesses have mostly welcomed the decision. The chamber of commerce said in a statement that shop owners on ⟦Harbor Street⟧ had long complained that customers struggled to find parking, and that better buses could ease the pressure. The ⟦Harlow Bay⟧ hospital, which employs about ⟦600⟧ people, said it would encourage staff to try the new routes and was considering offering discounted monthly passes.

Not everyone is convinced that people will change their habits. ⟦Ellen Marsh⟧, who teaches at the secondary school, said many of her colleagues live outside the town and would still need to drive. "It's great for people in the center," she said. "But if you live out past the quarry, none of this reaches you." The council has said that it will look at extending service to outlying areas in a second phase, though no timeline has been set.

The new bus routes are scheduled to start on September ⟦2⟧, the first day of the school year, while the additional ferry crossings will begin a month earlier, on August ⟦4⟧. ⟦Northgate Transit⟧ plans to publish the full timetables by the end of June and will hold drop-in sessions at the library for anyone who wants help planning their new journeys. Residents can also send questions to ⟦routes@northgate-transit.example.com⟧ or read the plan at ⟦https://harlowbay.example.org/transit⟧.

For Mayor ⟦Okafor⟧, the vote marks the end of a long argument but the start of a harder test. "We've made the decision," she said. "Now we have to prove it was the right one, and the only way to do that is to run a bus that people actually want to take."
