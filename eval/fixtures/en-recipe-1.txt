Smoky red lentil soup

This is the soup I make when the fridge is nearly empty and I want something warm without a trip to the shops. Red lentils cook quickly and break down into a thick, creamy base, so you don't need a blender or any cream. It keeps well for several days in the fridge and, like most soups, tastes even better the next day.

Serves ⟦4⟧ to ⟦6⟧

Ingredients
- ⟦2⟧ tablespoons olive oil
- ⟦1⟧ large onion, finely chopped
- ⟦2⟧ carrots, peeled and diced
- ⟦3⟧ cloves garlic, crushed
- ⟦1⟧ teaspoon ground cumin
- ⟦1⟧ teaspoon smoked paprika
- ⟦1⟧ tablespoon tomato paste
- ⟦250⟧ g red lentils, rinsed
- ⟦1.5⟧ liters vegetable stock
- ⟦1⟧ can (⟦400⟧ g) chopped tomatoes
- Juice of half a lemon
- Salt and black pepper
- A handful of fresh parsley or cilantro, to serve

Method

Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and carrots with a pinch of salt and cook, stirring now and then, for about ⟦10⟧ minutes, until the onion is soft and just starting to turn golden. Don't rush this part: it's where most of the flavor comes from.

Stir in the garlic, cumin and paprika and cook for another minute, until the spices smell fragrant. Add the tomato paste and keep stirring for a minute or two, so it darkens slightly and loses its raw taste.

Add the lentils, the stock and the chopped tomatoes. Bring everything to a boil, then lower the heat and let it simmer, partly covered, for ⟦20⟧ to ⟦25⟧ minutes. Stir from time to time, especially toward the end, because lentils like to stick to the bottom of the pot. The soup is ready when the lentils have completely fallen apart.

If you prefer a smooth soup, you can blend half of it and stir it back in, but I usually just give it a good whisk to break up the last few lentils. If it's too thick, add a splash of water; if it's too thin, let it bubble away for a few more minutes.

Finish with the lemon juice, then taste and season with salt and plenty of black pepper. The lemon is important, so don't skip it: it brightens everything up and stops the soup from tasting flat.

Serve in warm bowls with a scattering of chopped herbs, a drizzle of olive oil and some crusty bread on the side.
